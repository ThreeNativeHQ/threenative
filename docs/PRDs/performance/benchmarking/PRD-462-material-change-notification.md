# PRD-462: Material change notification instead of per-frame polling

**Status:** NOT STARTED
**Date:** 2026-09-28
**Target branch:** `develop`
**Owner request:** close the last row where Godot beats ThreeNative on the PRD-449 scoreboard, the same way a real game benefits.

## Problem

The render projection batches meshes whose materials differ only in colour into one instanced draw (`packages/core/src/projection-uniform.ts`). To honour "the game may change anything at any time", it polls every distinct member material every frame. That costs about 0.69 µs per material, or 2.8 ms at 4,096 materials, and it accounts for all of the remaining can't-batch gap. On the 2026-09-28 scoreboard, L4 ran 7.88 ms for TN against 5.95 ms for Godot at 4,096 cubes, and 2.08 against 1.73 ms at 1,024. A build without the check runs L4 at 4,096 in 2.8 ms. Agent-written games often create one material per object, so this is a real pattern, not a benchmark shape.

## Design (measured in a scratch micro-bench, 2026-09-28)

When a material joins a uniform batch, its watched own properties become accessor pairs that set a dirty bit on write. There is one shared accessor pair per key name, with values kept in a non-enumerable symbol store. The per-frame check becomes: dirty bit, then a component compare of the few object-valued fields (`Color`/`Vector2`/`Euler`), then the `defines` walk. That measured 0.056 µs per material, against 0.69 today. `onBeforeCompile` and `customProgramCacheKey` are watched by name. A material leaving the batch, or the batch being released, restores its plain data properties. The drawn material is the mirror's own plain clone, so three.js's draw path never reads a watched object.

- **Install cost:** about 32 µs per material, or 130 ms for 4,096. Watchers install in slices under a per-frame budget, and a member not yet watched stays on today's exact poll, so detection never lapses.
- **Game-side cost:** a watched material reads in about 24 ns instead of 1.5 ns, and writes in about 110 ns instead of 1 ns. This is off the draw path.
- **Accepted gaps (no guard row covers them today):** `delete material.<watched key>` and `Object.freeze` on a batched material. Both are recorded here and not silently claimed.

## Phases

### Phase 1: Change notification with the same guarantees

- [ ] Watch, dirty-check and restore in `projection-uniform.ts`, with release on every `projection-apply.ts` exit path (material swap, release, batch dispose, `releaseAll`). proof: the 14 existing `projection-uniform-batch.spec.ts` rows stay green; new rows red→green for `onBeforeCompile` and `customProgramCacheKey` assignment ejecting a member, data properties restored after ejection and after `releaseAll`, and a shared-lane material never watched.
- [ ] Budgeted, sliced install that falls back to the exact poll for members not yet watched. proof: a spec shows a member edited before its watcher installs is still ejected the same frame, and no frame installs past the budget.
- [ ] Core suite has no new failures. proof: `pnpm --filter @threenative/core exec vitest run`, compared with the known pre-existing set.

### Phase 2: Prove it on the GPU and on a real game

- [ ] L4@4,096 does not regress in the native arm. proof: `tn-desktop` runs B A B A B A with source hashes in each JSON, and L3 does not regress.
  **Not met, and it is now a measurement on a quiet machine rather than an inference from a loaded one.** Ten runs, `tn-notify-quiet-{before,after}-{1..4,gap}-2026-09-28.json`, B A B A B A B A, `--frames 400 --warmup 250` — 250 warmup against the 61 frames the 2 ms install budget needs to cover 4,096 materials, so every scored frame is past the install, and the 30-frame segments inside each run are flat, which is the check that it is. Every JSON carries `identity.sourceSha`: BEFORE `9afc61e0d7be` (origin/develop's two files), AFTER `2c4324898561` (HEAD's). The 1-minute load average read **3.35–4.77 before all ten runs and never reached the bar of 10**, so nothing was discarded for a busy machine. (The earlier six-pair A/B, `tn-notify-{before,after}-{1..6}-2026-09-28.json`, +1.88 at L4@4,096, was taken at load 10.8–102.6 and is superseded by this one; its JSONs stay as the record of that attempt.)
  Paired `after − before`, p50 frame ms, 4 pairs: **L4@4,096 +0.33 median** (`−1.01, −0.24, +1.00, +0.89`) — the BEFORE arm's own four runs span 1.68 ms, the AFTER arm's 0.91, the ranges overlap and the pairs disagree in sign; **L4@1,024 −0.24** (`−0.52, −0.15, −0.23, −0.24`, 4 of 4 negative, BEFORE 1.67–2.01 against AFTER 1.44–1.53, no overlap); **L3@4,096 +0.07**; **L3@1,024 +0.01**. The projection's own slice of the frame (`collapseMs`) moves with it: +0.30 median at L4@4,096, −0.21 at L4@1,024, +0.02 on both L3 rungs.
  **Verdict: REJECT.** The rung this PRD was filed on does not get faster — its median move is a fifth of the noise between two runs of the *same* build, and half its pairs point the other way. The 1,024 rung does get faster, by more than its own spread, and this PRD claims nothing about it. L3 does not regress at either count by more than its spread. So the premise — 0.69 µs a material, 2.8 ms at 4,096, "all of the remaining can't-batch gap" — does not survive contact with the arm: the poll is cheaper in situ than in a micro-bench because the objects it reads are already hot for the rest of the reconcile, and the watch trades a bulk copy for dictionary lookups that miss. The isolated numbers (1.04 µs polled → 0.22 µs watched, 4,096 members) are real and are in `projection-uniform-batch.spec.ts`; they are worth ~0 ms inside a real frame.
  **Where the settled L4@4,096 frame actually goes** (one extra run per arm with `TN_HOST_GAP_DETAIL=1`; `tn-notify-quiet-hostgap-settled-2026-09-28.json` carries every median this split quotes, the raw 300-sample arrays stay untracked): BEFORE, a 10.03 ms period is **9.10 ms inside the rAF callback (91%)** — 7.53 of it the projection reconcile, 75% of the whole frame, then 0.69 the game transform loop and 0.88 three.js render and submit — against 0.63 ms of native frame replay (6%), 0.10 present and 0.18 of everything else. AFTER is the same shape at 12.49: 11.34 JS, 0.73 replay, 0.10 present. **The frame is JS-side, the projection is most of it, and the native replay the mechanism was meant to relieve is 6% and unmoved in kind.** A cheaper drift check cannot pay for itself in a frame that is three-quarters its own caller, so the next attribution starts inside the 7.5 ms and not at the 0.6 ms.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The can't-batch rows are no longer Godot wins on the scoreboard, and every other row stays a TN win or tie. proof: the scoreboard JSONs and the report banner. — **not met, and not reachable by this change as landed.** The A/B above is the same measurement the can't-batch row is made of, and it says the poll was not what that row was losing to. The host-gap split above closes the guess this line used to make: there is no ~7 ms of L4@4,096 outside the reconcile to find. A 10.03 ms frame is 7.53 ms of reconcile, 0.88 of three.js render and submit, 0.69 of game step and 0.63 of native replay, so the row's remaining gap is inside the projection's own 7.5 ms and needs its own attribution, not a cheaper drift check.

## Decisions

**Site page waits for this PRD — owner, 2026-09-28.** The public benchmarks page on threenative-site ships once TN beats Godot on the can't-batch row, with charts and a "run it yourself" guide.

**The notification stays landed; the perf claim does not — owner rule, 2026-09-28.** Every guarantee the poll had is now proved by a spec row instead of by a per-frame scan (21 rows, the 14 originals among them), the classification no longer re-derives 4,096 fingerprints when one member moves, and the draw is byte-identical. What it does not do is make the frame faster, and the PRD's own acceptance is the frame. Rather than delete the mechanism, this PRD stays open with the number on it, and the next step is attributing the L4 frame that the number points at.
