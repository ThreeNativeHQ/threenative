| --- | --- | --- |
| Textures (72), engine-tracked | — | **379** |

## 2. Finding A — `GL mtrack` is real resident memory, not a `meminfo` categorisation artefact

The PRD's flowchart offered "meminfo artefact → document the real ceiling" as one of three
branches. It is **closed, against the artefact hypothesis**, by a second kernel instrument that
knows nothing about memtrack.

Lane: **physical Pixel 8**. Read through `run-as com.threenative.bayview` (the package is
debuggable, so no root was needed):

| Instrument | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `/proc/<pid>/status` `RssFile` | 979,560 kB |
| Sum of `Rss` over every file-backed VMA in `/proc/<pid>/smaps` | 125,744 kB |
| **Residual: file-resident pages `smaps` cannot see** | **853,816 kB** |
| `dumpsys meminfo` `GL mtrack` | 847,764 kB |
| **Agreement** | **within 6,052 kB — 0.7 %** |

Mechanism: the Mali driver maps its allocations through `/dev/mali0` with page flags the `smaps`
page-walk skips, so each of those VMAs honestly reports `Rss: 0`; the mm's own file-page counter,
which `/proc/<pid>/status` reports, counts them anyway. Two independent kernel paths therefore
agree that ~830 MiB of *physically resident* pages are held by the GPU driver on behalf of this
process.

Negative control, stated as the PRD requires: if `GL mtrack` were an artefact — memtrack
attributing memory that is not there, or double-counting something already in `Native Heap` —
then `RssFile` would be accounted for by the `smaps` file total and the residual would be near
zero. It is 853,816 kB. The artefact branch does not survive its own control.

Corroborating totals from the same capture: `smaps_rollup` `Rss` 571,464 kB against
`status` `VmRSS` 1,439,972 kB — the same ~850 MiB invisible to `smaps`, arrived at from the
other direction.

## 3. Finding B — the 2.11x is a **floor**, not a multiplier

This is the attribution the PRD asked for, and it lands on the "constant excess" branch.

Lane: **physical Pixel 8**. `GL mtrack` sampled against `TN_PRESENTS_TICK` from launch to steady
state. Abridged from `series-baseline.csv`; `t` is seconds after the sampler started, and the app
was launched immediately before `t=0`:

| t (s) | `GL mtrack` (kB) | engine textures | engine buffers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 491,608 | *(no tick yet)* | *(no tick yet)* |
| 6 | 700,692 | | |
| 9 | 701,048 | | |
| 12 | 771,232 | | |
| 15 | 834,296 | | |
| 18 | 837,584 | 379 MiB / 72 | 14 MiB |
| 29 | 845,300 | 379 MiB / 72 | 14 MiB |
| 58 | 848,264 | 379 MiB / 72 | 14 MiB |
| 77 | 847,764 | 379 MiB / 72 | 14 MiB |

Arithmetic:

- First sample: **491,608 kB = 480.1 MiB**, taken 1–2 s into the process. The logcat timeline
  puts the app at `TN_ANDROID_JS_NATIVE:{"calls":6,...,"draw":0,"drawIndexed":1}` at
  `21:04:25.6` — six binding calls and one pipeline. No asset texture exists yet.
- Growth from first sample to steady state: `847,764 − 491,608 = 356,156 kB = 347.8 MiB`.
- The game's entire engine-tracked request: **393 MiB**.
- **Growth / request = 0.885.**

The driver grew by **less than** what the game asked for. A 2.11x per-byte overhead is therefore
refuted: it would require growth of ~830 MiB over the load window, and the measured growth is
348 MiB. The 435 MiB excess is a fixed cost that is already in place before the game's textures
exist, plus a per-allocation component that is at most zero and measures slightly negative.

The steady state is also flat — 847,764 to 848,264 kB across 60 s of play, a 0.06 % band. That
independently re-confirms the bug doc's "not a growing leak" finding on a second run.

**Honest limits of Finding B.** I do not have an engine-byte reading at `t=2`, because
`TN_PRESENTS_TICK` first fires at frame 60 (~18 s in on this build). So "480 MiB before any
texture" rests on the logcat timeline rather than on a simultaneous marker, and is the weaker
half. The 0.885 growth ratio does not depend on it at all — both endpoints are measured — and
that is the half the conclusion rests on. Tightening the first half is queued as Ask A below.

### Why the `KILL_SHADOWS` caveat does not move either finding

Finding A compares two instruments reading the same process at the same instant; what the game
allocated is irrelevant to it. Finding B compares one run against itself, so shadow suppression
applies equally to both endpoints. And an unsuppressed build would request *more* than 393 MiB,
which lowers the 0.885 ratio further — i.e. it strengthens the conclusion rather than weakening
it. The caveat's real effect is on the absolute 379 MiB, which should be read as a floor.

### The engine's own counter is not trustworthy in either direction

Code-level, no device needed; read at `packages/runtime-native/src/webgpu/bindings.cpp` on this
worktree's HEAD. This is a real defect in the instrument the whole bug doc quotes:

1. **It is not a live figure.** `g_textureBytesLive` (line 295) and `g_bufferBytesLive`
   (line 370) are only ever incremented — the only occurrences are `+=` at lines 353 and 392, and
   there is no destroy hook anywhere in the file. Despite the name they are *cumulative created*.
   A game that streams and disposes textures will report a total that only ever rises.
   Direction: **over-reports** live bytes.
2. **It sees one of four texture-creation sites.** `recordTextureCreated` is called only at
   `bindings.cpp:4243` (the JS `device.createTexture` path). Not counted:
   `createLinearPresentationTexture()` at `bindings.cpp:883` (full-screen at surface format,
   ~9.9 MiB), `g_canvas2DTexture` at `bindings.cpp:5902` (full-screen RGBA8, ~9.9 MiB),
   `Context::createOffscreenTarget` at `context.cpp:546` (headless/desktop).
   Direction: **under-reports**.
3. **It sees one of five buffer-creation sites.** `recordBufferCreated` is called only at
   `bindings.cpp:2459`. Not counted: `g_screenshotBuffer` at `bindings.cpp:6129` and `6227`,
   `video/async_capture.cpp:113`, `video/gpu_readback_recorder.cpp:284`.
   Direction: **under-reports**.

So "393 MiB" should be read as *a create-cumulative count of JS-requested resources only*. It is
neither a live figure nor a whole-GPU figure. **This is the concrete Phase-2 engine item**, and it
is not fixed in this commit: it is a `packages/runtime-native` change, which needs its own
red-green, `pnpm census`, and a native build to verify — and the device it would be verified on is
leased to another lane. Filed here rather than guessed at.

## 4a. Ask C — the floor is **not** framebuffer-area-scaled

Lane: **physical Pixel 8**, executed by the PRD-214 lane during its Phase-0 bisect, from
`docs/verification/prd-213-piggyback-resolution-gl-mtrack.txt` at commit
`7e77021bdf808db1464f06132f81f60cd054ebb4` on `worktree-agent-a15fb02a370974a26`. Read with
`git show`; the worktree directory itself was never opened. 256 one-second samples.

The sweep is the negative control §3's floor needed. If the floor were the Mali tiler/JIT working
set, cutting the framebuffer to a quarter would cut it. Abridged, at the transitions:

| time | rung | `EGL mtrack` (kB) | `GL mtrack` (kB) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21:35:47 | full | 103,640 | 855,664 |
| 21:35:51 | full | 103,640 | 855,548 |
| **21:35:55** | **→ 50 %** | **64,780** | **921,280** |
| 21:36:15 | 50 % | 64,780 | 921,152 |
| **21:36:19** | **→ 25 %** | **55,120** | **921,164** |
| 21:36:44 | 25 % | 55,120 | 920,752 |
| **21:36:49** | **→ full** | **103,640** | **986,964** |
| 21:37:26 | full | 103,640 | 989,512 |

`EGL mtrack` tracks framebuffer area exactly, as §4 said it would. **`GL mtrack` does the
opposite**: it *rose* 64.2 MiB when the area was quartered, held flat while the area shrank
further, then rose another 64.7 MiB when the area was restored. It never fell when area fell.

**Conclusion: the floor is not the framebuffer-sized driver working set.** That hypothesis is
excluded. "A fixed wgpu or driver arena" is *consistent with* the data and is **not proved by
it** — these are three rungs of one process, not three cold launches, so the sweep can show
"does not shrink when area shrinks" and cannot show "is a constant". Naming the component needs
three cold launches at three fixed resolutions. Until then it stays unnamed.

## 4b. Boundedness — the title of this PRD says "and bounded", so this is not filed under the floor

The same series carries a second result that is about boundedness, not attribution, and it is
arguably the more serious of the two.

**At a fixed resolution, GPU memory is bounded.** Two independent runs agree: my own steady-state
sample held 847,764–848,264 kB across 60 s of ordinary play, a ±0.5 MB band, and the sweep's
half-resolution and quarter-resolution plateaus are flat to within 0.5 MB over 20–25 s each. The
bug doc's "bit-identical across samples" observation reproduces.

**Every surface resize adds ~64 MiB that is never returned.**

| transition | `GL mtrack` delta |
| --- | --- |
| full → 50 % | **+65,732 kB (+64.2 MiB)**, in a single one-second sample |
| 50 % → 25 % | −408 kB — **nothing** |
| 25 % → full | **+66,212 kB (+64.7 MiB)**, in a single one-second sample |

The middle row is what makes the other two readable: this is not "any resize costs 64 MiB". Two
transitions cost ~64 MiB each and one costs nothing, and the memory is never given back.

Why this matters beyond a bisect harness: **a phone resizes its surface on rotation, and on
entering or leaving multi-window.** A player who rotates the device a handful of times would, on
this evidence, lose several hundred MiB that never returns — into a process that already sits at
1.4 GiB `VmRSS` on a device that starts killing apps under 2 GiB. That is exactly the memory
pressure bug 4's SIGSEGV hypothesis needs, arrived at from a different direction.

What the signature fits, stated as a hypothesis and not as a finding: wgpu-hal suballocates
device memory through `gpu-alloc`, which pools freed blocks rather than returning them to Vulkan.
Under that model a resize destroys the old render targets, the pool keeps their memory, the new
sizes do not fit the freed holes, and a fresh chunk is taken from the driver — which is why the
kernel-level `GL mtrack` never falls. The step size being 65,732 and 66,212 kB, both a whisker
over exactly 64 MiB, is the kind of coincidence a power-of-two chunk allocator produces. **Not
proved**: confirming it needs a wgpu-side allocator instrument or a longer resize ladder.

This converges with the counter audit above rather than restating it. `g_textureBytesLive` never
decrements, and `createLinearPresentationTexture()` — framebuffer-sized, recreated on the resize
path — is one of the three uncounted texture sites. So the engine's own instrument would report
*nothing at all* for the largest single jump in GPU memory this project has measured. An
instrument that cannot see a 64 MiB step is the first thing to fix in Phase 2.

**A third, smaller term is open.** Across ~2 minutes of full-resolution play the sweep drifts
+7.8 MB (847,712 → 855,548) while my own run at a fixed resolution drifted ±0.5 MB. The
difference between the two runs is that 214's was switching materials and meshes per rung. So the
drift is most likely churn through the same pooling path rather than a time-based leak — but
"most likely" is not measured, and it is recorded as an open question with its measurement named:
a fixed-resolution run that creates and discards materials on a schedule, watching whether
`GL mtrack` steps or ramps.

## 5. The sky split — mechanism proved, device delta queued

**Layer: game.** `src/render/sky.ts` lives in the out-of-repo sandbox Bayview tree, and the batch
README names this as the one explicit exception to "no per-game workaround". No package code
decides anything here.

### Why one image was costing two

`three@0.185.1`, `build/three.webgpu.js`, read directly:

- `_setSizeFromTexture` (line 26218): for an equirect, `this._setSize(texture.image.width / 4)`.
- `_setSize` (line 26232): `_lodMax = floor(log2(cubeSize)); _cubeSize = 2 ** _lodMax`.
- `_allocateTarget` (line 26280): `width = 3 * max(_cubeSize, 112)`, `height = 4 * _cubeSize`.
- `_createRenderTarget` (line 26767): `type: HalfFloatType, format: RGBAFormat,
  generateMipmaps: false` → `rgba16float`, 8 bytes/texel, one mip.
- `_init` (line 26301) allocates a **ping-pong target of the same size** → exactly two.

So the image-based light is charged on the **source equirect's width**, in power-of-two steps.

### Executed, lane: desktop Chrome (`nvidia` / `turing`), real WebGPU

`sandbox/fps-framework/tools/pmrem-size-probe.mjs` drives the game's own installed three.js
against equirects of four widths and counts `GPUDevice.createTexture` bytes with the *same*
arithmetic `bindings.cpp` uses:

| equirect width | cubeUV target | PMREM pair | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3072 | 1536x2048 | `1536x2048 rgba16float` x2 | **48 MiB** |
| 2048 | 1536x2048 | `1536x2048 rgba16float` x2 | **48 MiB** |
| 1024 | 768x1024 | `768x1024 rgba16float` x2 | **12 MiB** |
| 512 | 384x512 | `384x512 rgba16float` x2 | **3 MiB** |

**Cross-lane corroboration.** The browser's 3072 row produces `1536x2048 rgba16float` x2 at
48 MiB — byte-identical to the `{"k":"1536x2048 rgba16float","n":2,"mb":48}` bucket measured on
the physical Pixel 8. The two lanes agree exactly at the one point where both were measured,
which is what licenses reading the other rows.

**The non-obvious result:** 3072 and 2048 cost the same. Halving the sky saves nothing; only
crossing a step does.

### What landed

- `sandbox/fps-framework/public/assets/bayview-sky-ibl.jpg` — 1024x512 Lanczos reduction of
  `bayview-sky.jpg` (3072x1536), 77 kB.
- `sandbox/fps-framework/src/render/sky.ts` — `setupSky(scene, background, environment?)`.
  `scene.background` keeps the full 3072 equirect (it is the thing the player looks at);
  `scene.environment` takes the 1024 copy. The `environment` parameter is optional: when it is
  omitted the background is reused for the light, which is exactly the pre-split behaviour, so
  the old two-argument call site keeps working unchanged.
- `sandbox/fps-framework/src/scenes/Play.ts` — loads and passes the second texture.
- Committed in the sandbox repository as `3c84631`, staged path by path so no other lane's
  uncommitted work was swept in.
- 1024 rather than 512 is deliberate and the reasoning is in the file: 512 would take another
  ~9 MiB but halves the mirror reflection again, and this scene has painted steel in it.
