This is a handoff, not a plan of record. It says what I found, what I changed, what I did not
finish, and what I would do first on the next session. Nothing here is a commitment made on
anyone else's behalf.

## The one thing to read first

For three consecutive rounds the loop reported `0/1` for both arms and nobody knew why.
It was not the games. `scripts/sweep-archive.ts` allowlisted `index.html` and `vite.config.*` at
the project root and silently dropped `threenative.config.ts`, which the starter's own
`src/game.ts` imports. **Every archived build 500'd in its dev server and never booted**, so the
proof runner reported a missing bridge for an application that had not started.

That means **every functional-column number before 2026-08-14 is void**, including the kill
switch round 4 fired. Fixed under PRD-107, with a resolution guard (`assertArchiveResolves`) that
fails the archive when `src/` imports a sibling the archive does not carry. The guard, not the
copy list, is the part that stops it recurring.

## Next actions, in the order I would do them

### 1. Run one paired round on the repaired instrument

This is the highest-value action and everything else can wait for it. No round has ever produced a
functional-column comparison, because until today no archive booted. A pair also settles two open
questions at once:

- **The deletion verdict on PRD-108.** Round 6 reached for `pushesDynamicBodies` and
  `linearVelocity` but **not** `applyImpulse` or `applyForce`. `AGENTS.md` rule 2 says an
  abstraction no fresh uninformed build reaches for is deleted in the round that discovers it. One
  round is thin evidence; a second unreached round is not. Delete them then, or keep them with the
  call site named.
- **Whether the round 6 fixes moved the score.** Axes 4 and 7 are stale on the current tree.

Run the vanilla arm too. `pnpm sandbox --arm vanilla` exists, and every score so far has carried an
*estimated* counterfactual, which is the weakest number in the whole record.

### 2. Decide the sealed-brief naming contract — this is blocking real measurement

The sealed proof pins entity ids (`player`, `crate`, `solid-body`, `goal`, `mission`) and
`world.seed = 6132` that `brief.md` never states, and the arm firewall forbids the builder from
reading the proof. So those assertions are **unpassable by any blind builder**.

Round 5 scored 2/10 and round 6 scored 0/10 on the same proof, and the difference was luck: round 5
happened to bind ArrowRight and name an entity `player`. That column currently measures naming
coincidence, not quality.

Two honest options, and it is an owner decision because changing a sealed input voids comparison
with every earlier round:

- State the ids and the seed in `brief.md`, so a builder can satisfy them without seeing the proof.
- Or rewrite the proof to assert observable behaviour rather than names.

I did not touch either. Recorded as gap 4 in `round-5-2026-08-14.md`.

### 3. Finish what PRD-109 deliberately left open

`rapier({ deterministicRestart: true })` makes a restarted scene reproduce its predecessor, proven
with a control test showing the default still diverges. It is **opt-in only** because freeing the
world leaves any retained `body.raw` pointing at freed WASM memory — `plugin.spec.ts` caught this
as `null pointer passed to rust`, and I made the option opt-in rather than weaken that test.

The real fix is handle invalidation: `dispose()` should clear `raw` so a stale reference reads
`undefined` instead of freed memory. That spans `handles.ts`, `simulation.ts` and `native/host.ts`,
and it would let determinism become the default. Worth doing before any game ships against the
option.

### 4. Make the scaffold's proof hook survive contact with a real game

**Both** round 5 and round 6 deleted all ten generated playtest scenarios and never used the
bridge. Round 6's builder was explicit: the scenarios assert the starter game's pickups, coyote
time and respawns, "none of which survives contact with a different game", and the `test` script
that runs them "is dead the moment you change the game".

This is the single largest unrealised value in the product. It costs points on two axes worth 35
combined, and it is why the sealed proof observed nothing in round 6 — only `entity: "goal"` was
ever registered.

Worth trying: ship **one** generic scenario that survives changing the game — boots, renders a
canvas, no console errors, the subject entity moves under input — and have the scaffold register
the player entity by default. That is a template change, not a package change.

### 5. Native side of PRD-108

`applyImpulse`/`applyForce`/`linearVelocity` and `pushesDynamicBodies` are web-proved and
native-guarded: the TypeScript ABI forwards and throws `TN_NATIVE_PHYSICS_ACTUATION_MISSING` on an
old runtime. The Rust `Simulation` has not gained the entry points, so **no native build can push a
crate yet**. Do not describe the physics work as done on native until that lands and runs.

## Smaller things I found and did not fix

- `IGameConfig.step` had no doc comment and the scaffold never sets it, which cost round 6's
  builder its "largest wrong turn" — a redundant accumulator that decoupled the character from the
  simulation. Documented under PRD-109. The scaffold still does not set `step`.
- The starter ships **two** HUD systems: `src/render/hud.ts` (instanced glyphs in the 3D scene) and
  `src/ui/Hud.tsx` (React). Both round 5 and round 6 deleted the 3D one.
- `RigidBody3D` requires an `Object3D` even for a collider-only static body, so a decorated wall
  group needs an empty anchor. Five lines of boilerplate per wall.
- An open engine question I could not settle from a code read, and deliberately did not guess at:
  round 5 measured that its `Area3D` never reported the `CharacterBody3D`, yet the platformer
  template's `Pickup` fires with a `CharacterBody3D` player and its `collect` playtest passes.
  `areaIntersections` uses `world.intersectionsWithShape`, which bypasses Rapier's
  `ActiveCollisionTypes`, so the obvious kinematic-pair explanation is wrong. Note the platformer's
  `Pickup` handler takes no body argument, so its passing test does not prove the player is what
  entered. Needs a targeted experiment before anyone writes a fix.
- Round 6's visual defects are **game-owned and should stay that way**: crates read as thin bright
  frames rather than solid painted wood, the character clips into the crate stack, and at 900×600
  the bottom hint bar overlaps the control text. The framework never owns the look; the lever is
  the scaffold's starting point or the game, never `packages/`.

## Two corrections to the record

Round 5's friction log asserted two things round 6 measured and disproved. Both were inferred
rather than instrumented, and the ledger has been updated:

- **Scene-created physics bodies are not disposed** — they are. Round 6 logged
  `ctx.physics.numBodies()` on scene entry and got `39` across three consecutive restarts.
- **No fixed-step option exists** — it does. `step` and `maxSteps` are in `IGameConfig`; the
  problem was that neither carried documentation.

The lesson is cheap to state and worth keeping: a friction log is a builder's experience, not a
measurement. Verify each claim against source or an instrument before turning it into a PRD.
