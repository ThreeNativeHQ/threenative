### Gap 1 — the fork, stated plainly

The glyph HUD is not redundant by accident. It is camera-attached Three.js geometry, so it is the
one that survives on native; the React HUD is web-only by construction. Round 9's rule applies:
a game that has to branch on platform is an engine bug wearing a game-code costume, so "render one
on web and the other on native" is not an acceptable template-level answer.

| Shape | What ships | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — one HUD, like `starter`** | Drop the glyph HUD from both templates. All seven templates then have a single React HUD, which is the shape `starter`'s own AGENTS.md documents: gameplay and state live in the scene, and a native HUD is the user's to add "only if your game needs one" | `platformer` claims web *and desktop*, and desktop is native. It would ship with no HUD there. That is a real capability loss, stated rather than hidden |
| **B — one HUD, the portable one** | Drop the React HUD from both templates and keep the glyph HUD | Keeps native parity, and costs the legibility: the blind critic read the crisp React row as the good one and the glyph block as the mush. It also makes two templates diverge from `starter`'s documented shape |

Recorded without choosing, because either choice changes what every user of those two templates
receives, and neither is a defect fix that can be made on the round's own authority.

## Notes

**What round 9's fix did and did not do.** The sky-dome `fog: false` fix landed the day before and
these are the first frames after it. `defense.png` went from 17.9 KB to 169 KB and `minimal.png`
from 34.2 KB to 97.4 KB, because a near-uniform image compresses to almost nothing. Both still
score 2, for the unrelated reasons above. The fix was necessary and nowhere near sufficient, and
that is exactly the kind of thing a baseline exists to say out loud.

**The instrument's own limit.** One still per template, no motion, no interaction. The critic scored
`playability` from HUD readouts and said so. A template that reads well in a frame and plays badly
would score well here. Round 9's judge recorded the same limit; two rounds is enough to call it a
known property of the instrument rather than a caveat to re-derive.

**A guard false-positived and was not weakened.** `pnpm sweep:judge` rejected the verdict
`TN_JUDGE_VOID: critic input contains an arm identifier` because the critic wrote "control bar",
meaning a row of input prompts, and `\bcontrol\b` is reserved. The critic reworded the phrase; every
score was preserved; the guard is unchanged. It will recur, because "control" is ordinary
vocabulary for describing a HUD, and the guard is being reused slightly off-label — a template
baseline has no arms to keep apart.

## Re-measure — the verdict, and one change that backfired

Same command, same harness, fresh critic that was told not to read the earlier verdict. Pre-fix
scores preserved at `docs/verification/visuals/baseline-pre-fix/`.

| Template | Before | After | Δ | Changed this round? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| shooter | 2 | **4** | **+2** | yes — doubled HUD removed |
| defense | 2 | 3 | +1 | yes — gradient sky |
| minimal | 2 | 2 | 0 | no |
| action-rpg | 4 | 3 | −1 | **no — nothing was touched** |
| platformer | 3 | 2 | −1 | yes — fog range |
| racing | 3 | 2 | −1 | yes — gradient sky |
| starter | 4 | **2** | **−2** | yes — landmark replaced |

At floor: 2/7 → 1/7. Mean 2.86 → 2.57.

**Read the calibration row before reading anything else.** `action-rpg` was not touched by any
change in this round and moved a full point between the two critics. That is the noise floor of
this instrument as currently run — one rater, one still, no repeat — and it means **a ±1 move here
is not evidence of anything.** Only the two-point moves survive it.

### What is proved

**The doubled HUD was the single worst defect in the set, and removing it is worth two points.**
`shooter` went 2 → 4 and its polish average 2.0 → 3.6, from worst-tier to the only frame at the
floor. Both critics named the same mechanism independently: the first read its upper-left quarter
as interleaved unreadable glyphs, the second scored the fixed frame as the strongest of the seven.
That is the round's real result.

### What backfired, and it was mine

**`starter` went 4 → 2, and the cause is the change I made to it.** The critic: "a stock
lathe-turned pawn several times everything else's height floats above the platform with no contact
shadow and is the largest thing in frame."

Both faults are mine and neither is a matter of taste. The old torus-knot was centred on its own
origin, so `position.set(-2, 2.6, -1.5)` read as mounted; my replacement stacks upward from its
base, so the same position left it hanging in the air. And I sized it against nothing. I replaced a
stock primitive with a bigger, floating, unshadowed one, in the template that was previously the
best of the seven.

**Reverted.** Gap 4 is recorded as attempted, regressed and withdrawn. The observation that a
stock primitive is a weak subject stands — the first critic named it unprompted — but it was a
one-point observation inside the noise floor, and I traded a measured 4 for a measured 2 chasing
it. A visual change made without looking at the result is a guess, and the fact that this loop's
whole purpose is to stop that makes it worse, not more forgivable.

### What is not attributable

`defense` +1, `platformer` −1 and `racing` −1 are all inside the noise floor. Specifically:

- `platformer`'s fog range moved from 28 to 90 and the critic still reports "mid-ground fogged to
  the background colour". Either the range is still short for that level, or the wash is exposure
  rather than fog. **Unresolved**, and the fix is not claimed to have worked.
- `racing`'s new complaint is that "the camera is rolled about 90 degrees — the horizon runs
  vertically down the right edge and the track reads as a wall". Nothing in this round touched its
  camera, and the previous critic described the same framing failure in gentler terms. It is a real
  defect and it is not one this round caused or fixed.

### A defect the fix missed, now closed

The second critic found `minimal` still drawing two score readouts — "a small Score: 0 chip
overlapping a large glowing SCORE 0". `minimal` ships no React, so it slipped through the first
version of the no-double-HUD invariant, which only compared a React mount against a geometry mount.
It was drawing a DOM `#score` chip from `main.ts` on top of the geometry HUD. The DOM chip is gone,
the invariant now counts DOM, React and geometry layers alike, and it fails when any template
reaches two.

### The committed frames are the scored frames, not the current tree

`docs/verification/visuals/*.png` are the frames the second critic scored. Two changes landed
*after* that capture — the landmark revert and `minimal`'s DOM-chip removal — so those two frames
show a state the source no longer produces. They are kept as they are because they are the evidence
behind the scores above; a frame recaptured after the fact would not be the thing that was judged.
Round 11 recaptures both conditions together.

### What round 11 must change about the instrument

One rater, one still, one run per template is not enough to act on a one-point move, and this round
spent real work on moves that size. Before the next comparison: fix the rater, or use three and take
a median, and score the same frames twice to measure the rater's own spread. A number the loop
cannot distinguish from noise is a number the loop should not be optimising.
